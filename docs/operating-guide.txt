THE AI LAYER (new)
------------------
What it does: estimates battery state of health WITHOUT a capacity test,
from discharge voltage, cell temperature and cycle count. Explains every
estimate (SHAP: health points each input cost), projects cycles left
until 80%, and gives Normal / Warning / Critical.

Health is the model's OUTPUT, never an input. (The old Edge Impulse
model took health as an input and its labels were health bands, so a
single if-statement matched it 88% of the time. That is fixed.)

Files
  health_ai.py            shared logic: model, SHAP, reasons, verdict
  train_health_model.py   trains + validates; writes the files below
  health_inference.py     live AI - reads battery/state, writes battery/ai
  demo_nasa_replay.py     replays an unseen NASA battery for the demo
  nasa_battery_cycles.csv the NASA training data
  health_model.pkl        the trained model           (made by training)
  validation_report.txt   the numbers for your slides (made by training)
  validation_report.png   AI vs lab on every unseen battery - PUT THIS
                          ON A SLIDE

Retired: ei_inference.py, scaling.json, model.tflite, demo_ageing.py,
cycle_history.json. Delete them if you like; nothing uses them now.


TESTING YOUR OWN CELLS (the "ground work")
------------------------------------------
Goal: check the AI on real used cells against a real capacity test.

Safety first: reject any cell that is swollen, dented, rusted, leaking
or resting below 2.5 V. Charge only with a proper Li-ion charger, on a
non-flammable surface, with someone watching. Never heat cells to age
them faster.

For each cell:
  1. Charge fully (4.2 V, until charge current falls below ~0.05 C).
  2. Rest 30 minutes.
  3. Discharge at about 1 C (2-2.5 A for a 2-2.5 Ah cell) down to 3.0 V
     with a capacity tester, a temperature probe taped to the cell.
  4. Write down: capacity (Ah), energy (Wh), average cell temperature.
       soh         = measured Ah / rated Ah from the cell's label
       voltage     = Wh / Ah  (that IS the mean discharge voltage)
       temperature = average of the probe readings
  5. Add a row to my_cells_template.csv (rename it my_cells.csv).
     Unknown history? put cycle = 0 and train with --no-cycle.

ON YOUR OWN PACK (the rig)
  1. python start_all.py   (real mode - the bridge logs everything)
  2. Full charge, rest 30 min, then discharge at about 1 C until the
     pack cuts off. Press "Cycle done" in the control app.
  3. python cell_test_report.py      (add --rated X if not 3.7 Ah)
     -> measured health vs AI estimate, the reasons, and a PNG.
     Add --save to keep the result in my_cells.csv.
  If it prints "outside the training range", the AI is being asked
  about conditions NASA never tested (e.g. your discharge stops at
  3.0 V/cell, NASA's went to 2.7 V). Say so - and that is exactly why
  retraining on your own cells (--extra) and a second dataset matters.

Then:
  python train_health_model.py --extra my_cells.csv --no-cycle

Every cell is tested by a model that never saw it, and the report shows
AI estimate vs your measured capacity, cell by cell.


THE AI ON THE CHIP (single ESP32, firmware Rev B)
-------------------------------------------------
The trained model is converted to C and compiled into the ESP32, so the
chip estimates health and explains it by itself (no laptop, no cloud).
Results go to battery/ai_edge and appear on the dashboards as a dashed
purple line on top of the laptop's orange one.

  1. python train_health_model.py   (also writes the model for the chip:
       firmware/EV_SINGLE_ESP32/health_model.h - must say
       "max difference 0.0000" or a tiny number)
  2. Arduino IDE -> File -> Open -> firmware/EV_SINGLE_ESP32/EV_SINGLE_ESP32.ino
     (health_model.h must stay in the same folder)
  3. Set WIFI_SSID / WIFI_PASSWORD / MQTT_HOST at the top (they are
     placeholders in the repo)
  4. CHECK ACS712_SENS_V_PER_A against the chip printed on your ACS712:
       5A = 0.185   20A = 0.100   30A = 0.066 (current value)
  5. Upload. Serial Monitor (115200) shows at boot:
       AI model: XGBoost, 300 trees, compiled into this firmware
       AI self-test: new-cell estimate ~99% in NN us
  6. Demo: python start_all.py demo  (the ESP32 can stay powered - the
     replay sends DEMO_ON, the board goes quiet and runs its AI on the
     replay; Serial prints one "[EDGE AI] cycle ..." line per cycle)
     On the real pack it prints one line per discharge (5 min or longer).

TWO-WAY CONTROL (twin -> pack)
------------------------------
  Commands on battery/cmd/sys:
    LIMIT:<amps> / LIMIT:OFF   cap the load current
    FAN:<0-100> / FAN:AUTO     fan override / temperature curve
    SYNC                       SOC from resting voltage
    DEMO_ON / DEMO_OFF         sent by the replay and the simulator
  Automatic: on a RESISTANCE JUMP the twin sends LIMIT:0.5 and FAN:100
  and logs a TWIN_ACTION event. Clear it with "Clear twin limit" in the
  control app (port 8503) once the cause is found.
  Leaving demo mode clears any limit the simulated twin set.

KALMAN FILTER (state estimation, in twin_engine.py)
---------------------------------------------------
Every second: PREDICT state of charge from the current, then CORRECT it
with the measured voltage. Learns internal resistance (R0) as it goes.
  * fixes coulomb-counting drift and a wrong starting SOC
  * reports its own uncertainty (SOC 63% +/- 0.4%)
  * keeps the physics twin in sync, so the residual only shows real faults
  * RESISTANCE JUMP alarm when R0 rises 20% within 5 minutes (needs 10
    minutes of running first, while it learns the pack)

  python test_kalman.py       proof on a simulated cell -> kalman_test.png
  python start_all.py fault   rehearse the alarm with no hardware

  IMPORTANT: set capacity_Ah in twin_engine.py (PARAMS) to your cells'
  rated capacity - the filter's SOC depends on it.
